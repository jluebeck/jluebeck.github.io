Why it matters

Cancer often copies its oncogenes onto circles of DNA that live outside the chromosomes.

These circles, ecDNA, have no centromere, so they are split unevenly every time a cell divides. One daughter cell can inherit dozens of extra oncogene copies and the other almost none. That lets a tumour change faster than chromosomes would allow. ecDNA is found in about 17% of tumours and is linked to drug resistance and shorter survival.

You can't target what you can't see. Sequencing reads arrive as short, scrambled fragments, and recovering a circle's structure from them is a graph problem. My work turns that problem into tools that run on real patient data, at scale, in other people's hands.

Software

AmpliconSuite

Fifteen interoperable open-source tools, from raw reads to a classified, visualised amplicon. I founded the project and lead its development.

AmpliconSuite-pipelinelead
One command from sequencing data to detected and classified amplicons.
AmpliconClassifierlead
Calls ecDNA, breakage-fusion-bridge and other amplification types.
AmpliconRepository.orgproject lead
The largest community-editable collection of ecDNA annotations.
AmpliconArchitectmaintainer
Builds the breakpoint graph of an amplified region from short reads.
AmpliconReconstructorlead
Joins sequencing with optical maps to resolve full structures.
CoRALco-developer
Reconstructs ecDNA from long-read sequencing.
CycleVizlead
Draws circular and rearranged genome structures.
ecSimulatorlead
Simulates ecDNA structures for benchmarking.
