Why it matters

Some of the most aggressive cancers keep their oncogenes off the chromosomes.

ecDNA appears in about 17% of tumours and is linked to drug resistance and shorter survival. It is also hard to see.

Outside the chromosomes

Oncogenes get copied onto small circles of DNA that float free in the nucleus, sometimes hundreds per cell.

Inherited unevenly

With no centromere, the circles split randomly at cell division. Tumours can raise or drop oncogene dose within a few generations.

Hard to see in the data

Sequencing returns millions of short fragments. Proving a circle exists, and what it contains, means solving a graph reconstruction problem.

AmpliconSuite

From raw reads to a reconstructed circle.

I founded AmpliconSuite and lead its development: 15 interoperable open-source tools for analysing focal amplifications across sequencing technologies.

  1. DetectAmpliconSuite-pipeline

    Finds amplified regions in whole-genome sequencing, one command end to end.

  2. ReconstructAmpliconArchitect · CoRAL

    Builds a breakpoint graph from short or long reads and walks it for cycles.

  3. ClassifyAmpliconClassifier

    Separates ecDNA from breakage-fusion-bridge and other amplifications.

  4. ShareCycleViz · AmpliconRepository.org

    Draws the structures and publishes them in a searchable, community-edited database.
